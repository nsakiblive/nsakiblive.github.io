01 / THE BRIEF

The information existed.
The work was making it usable.

A protein record.
A structural model.
A paper.
One connected research trail.

Research on tardigrade stress-protection proteins is distributed across protein databases, structure repositories, journals and supplementary archives.

I needed to locate the underlying assets around Dsup, CAHS and SAHS, verify what each source contained, retrieve useful files and organise them so another researcher could follow the evidence without repeating the discovery process.

MY CONTRIBUTION

Scientific data discovery, source verification, evidence organisation and research-ready packaging. The biological experiments and computational studies are the work of the credited researchers.

02 / WHY TARDIGRADES

Extreme survival.
Different molecular questions.

Tardigrades can survive conditions that put severe stress on DNA, proteins, membranes and cellular systems. Their resilience brings several kinds of protective biology into the same research landscape.

Dsup research follows DNA-associated protection. CAHS studies examine cytosolic responses to water loss and molecular assembly. SAHS studies investigate secretory and extracellular protection.

Understanding that landscape meant keeping these evidence tracks distinct, including the species, stress conditions and experimental system behind each result.

06 / BEYOND DSUP

The landscape widened
as the evidence deepened.

DNA repair is another part of the story.

Research in H. exemplaris found a strong inducible DNA-repair response after ionizing radiation. RNA interference targeting XRCC5 reduced irradiated survival, making repair a functional evidence track in its own right.

That result belongs alongside the Dsup literature with its organism and experimental context preserved.

Inspect the repair-response figure ↗

CAHS: assembly under stress.

CAHS D studies connect reversible protein assembly with stabilisation and biostasis. The package links sequence and model records with material behaviour and functional experiments.

SAHS: a different protective environment.

SAHS research examines secretory proteins and protection of membrane-containing structures during desiccation. These proteins have their own structural and experimental trail.

08 / CURATION DECISIONS

Research depth.
Retrieval discipline.

01

Follow the identifier.

Protein accessions and original record headers provide a more reliable anchor than a local filename. Sequence, model and experiment remain separate evidence types.

02

Preserve the source trail.

The README maps repositories and publications to the files. Publication figures retain study, organism and figure-number context when presented here.

03

Keep the core package usable.

Directly relevant, inspectable files take priority. Large external omics archives can remain source references rather than expanding the core delivery indiscriminately.

04

Separate copies from coverage.

A file can appear under more than one research track. The portfolio inventory distinguishes packaged files from byte-identical copies so file count does not overstate evidence breadth.

The supplied archive contains 143 files. For this portfolio, every file was indexed by path, byte size and SHA-256 so the collection can be inspected without downloading the full archive.
