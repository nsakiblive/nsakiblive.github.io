Making a field visit useful means translating a place, not simply showing it.

01 / THE ASSIGNMENT

Make the visit
more than a tour.

Women’s groups connected with Prakriti Resources Centre (PRC), Nepal came to Bangladesh for hands-on exposure to climate action and community realities. The value of the visit depended on more than moving people from one site to another.

Because I had already spent substantial time working in Mongla with ICCCAD, my role was to help supervise and coordinate the visit, guide the group through the local context, and explain what climate vulnerability looks like when it is lived every day.

03 / WHY MONGLA MATTERED

Climate vulnerability here is
not an abstract condition.

The article describes Mongla through sea-level rise, salinity intrusion, cyclones, tidal surges, erosion and scarcity around water, arable land, healthcare, education and employment. In field conversations, the gendered burden became equally important: high salinity and limited safe water shape women’s everyday health and hygiene realities in ways that broad climate indicators can miss.

04 / THE FIELD EXCHANGE

01

Read the local context

Ground the visit in the coastal pressures communities were already navigating.

02

Meet local institutions

The visiting group met the Mayor of Mongla and discussed women’s empowerment, resources and cultural barriers.

03

Connect adaptation practice

The group visited the Local Adaptation Centre and learned about the Climate Resilient Migrant Friendly Town work.

04

Listen to women locally

At Ratarati Colony, conversations with women leaders brought health, water and climate pressures into the exchange in direct terms.

05

Debrief and share back

The exchange continued in Dhaka, where the PRC group and ICCCAD team shared learning across their respective climate work.

05 / WHAT THE EXCHANGE MADE VISIBLE

Peer learning works when
lived experience stays in the room.

The exchange was not about presenting Bangladesh as a model to copy. It was about making comparison possible: what could participants from Nepal learn from Mongla, what needed to remain context-specific, and what could travel across borders?

Women’s local knowledge made that comparison more concrete. Climate vulnerability was visible not only in hazards, but in water access, health, livelihoods, mobility, household burdens and the limits communities face when trying to adapt.

“When women are empowered, positive outcomes follow.”Climate Tribune · June 2023

08 / REFLECTION

Good field coordination is partly logistics.
The more valuable part is interpretation.

People can visit the same place and leave with very different levels of understanding. The work is to help them notice the relationships between what they see, what communities say, and the wider systems shaping those realities.
